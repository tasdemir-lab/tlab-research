## Core Question

Does the paper make its argument in the right order, with the right emphasis, and with claims that are matched to evidence?

## Observed Journal Norms

Across the expanded sample above, strong journal articles usually do the following:

- State the question, design, main finding, and contribution early in the introduction rather than forcing the reader to infer the paper's point.
- Preview the actual evidence and headline results, not just the topic or motivation.
- Position the paper against the closest literature in a contribution-focused way, often inside the introduction itself.
- Move from question to framework/setting/data/design before asking the reader to absorb large result blocks.
- Keep the main text centered on score-moving evidence; appendices carry overflow detail, but the main paper still contains the core validation needed to trust the design.
- Separate baseline results from mechanism, heterogeneity, and auxiliary exercises instead of blending all findings into one diffuse narrative.
- End with a short, calibrated conclusion that restates what was learned, where the result is local, and what remains unresolved.

The sampled papers also show meaningful variation:

- `AER`, `QJE`, `JPE`, and `AEJ: Applied` tolerate long introductions if those pages are doing real argumentative work.
- `AEJ: Applied` and `Labour Economics` are usually less tolerant of ornamental setup and more likely to move briskly into design, data, and estimating variation.
- `QJE` and `JPE` more readily support a longer conceptual or framework build if it clearly sharpens the estimand, mechanism, or interpretation.
- Some papers use a short dedicated framework or setting section before the main design; others fold this material into the introduction. What matters is argumentative necessity, not stylistic purity.

## Mini-Norms By Paper Type

Use these subtype norms before deciding that a paper is structurally weak. A paper can be well organized for its design even if it does not resemble another design's ideal template.

### 1. Experimental Papers

- Experimental papers usually identify the intervention, treatment variation, sample, and primary outcome logic almost immediately.
- The introduction often previews the intervention and headline treatment effects earlier and more concretely than reduced-form papers do.
- Design credibility often comes from protocol clarity, treatment take-up or compliance discussion, outcome measurement, and pre-specification or experimental discipline rather than from a long institutional narrative.
- Mechanism sections usually come after baseline treatment effects and after the paper has established that the intervention moved the intended object.
- Experimental papers often justify why the setting matters for a broader economic question; the best ones do this without pretending the external-validity problem is solved.

### Experimental Failure Modes

- The paper spends pages motivating the topic before saying what the intervention actually is.
- Treatment effects arrive before the reader understands assignment, compliance, or the effective estimand.
- A field experiment is written as if it delivers universal policy guidance when the setting-specific margin is narrow.
- The paper buries heterogeneity between intention-to-treat, treatment-on-the-treated, and behavioral mechanism claims.

### 2. Reduced-Form Empirical Papers

- Reduced-form papers usually earn reader trust by stating the source of identifying variation early and by saying why that variation is plausibly informative.
- Their introductions often move quickly from puzzle to institutional setup to quasi-experimental variation to headline estimates.
- Main-text structure typically puts identification, baseline results, and decisive validation before mechanism, welfare, or broad policy extrapolation.
- Strong reduced-form papers often use the introduction itself to delimit the claim: what is local, what is causal, and what is only suggestive.
- Robustness carries more structural weight here than in many experimental papers because design credibility is often the main editorial question.

### Reduced-Form Failure Modes

- The paper hides the source of variation behind broad motivation or literature summary.
- Institutional detail is extensive but does not clearly map to the identifying assumption.
- Mechanism, welfare, or general-equilibrium language outruns the reduced-form evidence.
- The paper imitates top-journal cadence while the main evidentiary package still looks incremental or weakly defended.

### 3. Theory-Guided Empirical Papers

- Theory-guided empirical papers usually earn extra introduction space when it clarifies the mechanism, target estimand, or comparative statics that discipline the empirics.
- The framework section should narrow the reader's interpretation problem, not merely display sophistication.
- Results sections should map estimates back to the conceptual framework, often by showing which predictions survive, which channels are ruled out, and which remain observationally similar.
- These papers can sustain a slightly longer bridge from question to evidence, but only if the bridge is doing real conceptual work.
- Conclusions are often strongest when they restate both what the theory helped organize and what the empirics still do not pin down.

### Theory-Guided Failure Modes

- The framework is detached from the estimating object and could be deleted without changing the empirical interpretation.
- The paper invokes theory to overclaim what a reduced-form result means.
- Formal structure appears early, but the later evidence never returns to the model's key predictions.
- The paper confuses having a model with having evidence that discriminates among mechanisms.

## What To Check

## 1. Introduction Discipline

- Does the introduction state the question early?
- Does it identify the design or evidence clearly?
- Does it preview the main finding, not just the topic?
- Does it explain what is new relative to the closest papers?
- Does it distinguish contribution from motivation?
- Does it use early paragraphs to do substantive argumentative work, as in the sampled `AER`, `QJE`, `JPE`, and `AEJ: Applied` papers, rather than filling space with generic motivation?
- Is the introduction calibrated to the right subtype norm, or is it being judged against the wrong paper form?

### Failure modes

- The introduction sells the topic rather than the paper.
- The contribution claim is broad, but the evidence supports only a narrower claim.
- The main result appears too late.
- The introduction promises mechanism, welfare, or policy relevance that the paper never really delivers.
- The introduction is long but low-yield: many pages of scene-setting, little design, little evidence, and no clear editorial reason to keep reading.

## 2. Section Logic

- Does each major section answer a necessary question in the overall argument?
- Is the sequence natural: question, setting, design, data, results, interpretation?
- Does the ordering resemble the persuasive logic seen in the sampled papers: introduction, then whatever framework/setting/data/design is needed before main results, then interpretation/extensions, then conclusion?
- Are background or institutional sections too long relative to the empirical leverage they provide?
- Are later sections earning their place, or are they decorative add-ons?

### Failure modes

- The paper feels like two papers glued together.
- The theory, institutional background, and empirics are each competent but poorly integrated.
- A mechanism or heterogeneity section appears because it is fashionable, not because the paper needs it.
- Secondary sections distract from the main identification or contribution.
- The paper delays the design or key evidence too long because it treats background as a display of knowledge rather than a tool of persuasion.

## 3. Claim-Evidence Matching

- For each major claim, can you point to the evidence that supports it?
- Does the conclusion say more than the results justify?
- Are policy claims proportional to the design?
- Are mechanism claims stronger than the mechanism evidence?
- Are literature-positioning claims stronger than the actual distinction from nearby work?

### Failure modes

- Evidence supports a local result, but the prose implies a general result.
- The paper moves from reduced-form evidence to welfare language too quickly.
- The conclusion sharpens the claim instead of narrowing it.
- The abstract is stronger than the paper.
- The introduction or conclusion sounds like a `QJE` or `AER` paper, but the underlying evidence package is closer to a field-journal incremental paper.

## 4. Narrative Focus

- Is the paper organized around one main contribution, or does it keep shifting the reason the reader should care?
- Are the key tables and figures aligned with the main story?
- Are secondary findings clearly secondary?
- Is there one dominant storyline, or several competing ones?

### Failure modes

- The paper uses different contribution claims in the abstract, introduction, results, and conclusion.
- Heterogeneity results quietly replace the main result as the selling point.
- The paper tries to be about contribution, policy, mechanism, and method all at once.
- The main text keeps promoting appendix-grade exercises until the central claim becomes hard to identify.

## 5. Internal Transitions

- Do section openings and closings explain why the next section is needed?
- Does the empirical section answer the questions raised by the setup and theory?
- When a surprising result appears, does the paper pause to interpret it?

### Failure modes

- The reader can follow individual sections but not the full argument.
- Tables appear before the paper explains why they matter.
- Mechanism discussion arrives without prior motivation.
- The conclusion feels detached from the body.

## 6. Length And Compression

- Is the paper too long for the contribution it makes?
- Are long literature or institutional sections doing real argumentative work?
- Could the paper become sharper by cutting or merging sections?

### Failure modes

- The paper is 20 percent too long because it repeats the same contribution claim in several forms.
- Appendix-worthy detail crowds the main text.
- Important caveats are buried while low-value exposition stays in the main paper.

## Journal-Calibrated Checks

Use the sampled-paper patterns above as referee checks, not as rigid format rules.

### A. Introduction As Editorial Pitch

- In the sampled `AER`, `QJE`, `JPE`, and `AEJ: Applied` papers, the introduction quickly tells the editor what the paper does, how it does it, what it finds, and why that changes the literature.
- Penalize introductions that need several pages before the design or main result becomes visible.
- Penalize introductions that substitute topical importance for contribution.
- For experimental papers, penalize introductions that delay the intervention and treatment contrast.
- For theory-guided empirical papers, allow somewhat more conceptual setup only when it materially sharpens interpretation.

### B. Early Placement Of Design-Critical Material

- Strong papers do not hide the core design logic behind long institutional or literature tours.
- If the reader cannot assess credibility until far into the paper, structure is part of the problem, not just exposition.
- A short framework or setting section is fine when it disciplines interpretation, as in the sampled `AEJ: Applied` and `QJE` papers. It is not fine when it merely delays the evidence.
- In reduced-form papers, this usually means the source of variation and main threat are visible very early.
- In experimental papers, this usually means assignment, treatment, and outcome logic are visible very early.

### C. Baseline Before Ornament

- Baseline identification and headline results should arrive before long mechanism, heterogeneity, or auxiliary sections.
- If the paper needs a mechanism section to make the main result feel interesting, the contribution may be underpowered or misframed.
- If the robustness case is central to credibility, at least the decisive checks should appear in the main text rather than being outsourced entirely to the appendix.
- In theory-guided papers, the conceptual framework may come before the baseline results, but it still has to earn that placement by disciplining what the reader should look for in the evidence.

### D. Literature Positioning As Argument, Not Ritual

- The sampled top-journal papers position themselves against nearby papers with specific contrasts, not broad "this relates to" lists.
- Ask whether the literature discussion actually changes how the reader understands the contribution.
- Penalize literature sections that are long but strategically empty.

### E. Conclusion Discipline

- Strong conclusions are short, concrete, and narrower than the introduction when the evidence is local.
- Penalize conclusions that add policy ambition, mechanism certainty, or external-validity scope that the main paper never earned.
- Reward conclusions that tell the reader exactly what was learned and what remains unsettled.

## High-Value Referee Questions

- If I cut this section, would the core paper become clearer or weaker?
- What is the one sentence the paper wants the reader to remember, and does the manuscript consistently support it?
- Is the paper's main claim the same in the abstract, introduction, results, and conclusion?
- What is the first point at which the paper overclaims?
- Is the problem really weak evidence, or weak argument architecture?
- Does the paper look structurally like a strong journal article because it is well organized, or only because it imitates the surface features of one?
- Is the paper being benchmarked against the correct empirical subtype, or against a stronger journal template from a different design?

## What Strong Papers Usually Do

- State the question, design, main finding, and contribution early.
- Use the introduction as a real argumentative section, not as ceremonial throat-clearing.
- Build sections in a sequence that mirrors the logic of persuasion.
- Use theory, institutional context, or technical primer material to discipline the empirics rather than decorate them.
- Keep the main text focused on what the reader must know to assess the contribution and trust the design.
- Separate baseline results from mechanism, heterogeneity, and secondary exercises.
- Narrow claims when the evidence is local or incomplete.

## Upgrade Advice Patterns

- Narrow and sharpen the introduction so it previews the actual contribution rather than the broad topic.
- Move design-critical material earlier if the reader cannot judge the paper's credibility until too late.
- Cut or move sections that do not change the editorial judgment.
- Reorder sections so the evidence needed for the next claim appears before the claim is made.
- Separate the main claim from mechanism or policy extensions.
- Pull the decisive validation or robustness exercise into the main text if the current structure makes trust depend on appendix archaeology.
- Rewrite the conclusion to match the paper's actual evidentiary reach.
