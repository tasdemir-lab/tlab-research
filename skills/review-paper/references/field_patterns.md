# Field Patterns

Load this file when the paper sits in a specific applied field and the review needs sharper expectations about what referees usually push on.

## How To Use

- Use this file to sharpen, not replace, the main rubric.
- Do not force a field template onto a paper that clearly belongs elsewhere.
- Prefer the closest substantive field and borrow adjacent checks only when they are genuinely relevant.

## Labor

### Common referee pressure points

- Selection into employment, occupation, hours, or firm type.
- Equilibrium or spillover effects when treatment changes local labor demand or supply.
- Intensive versus extensive margin interpretation.
- Wage, earnings, and employment outcomes being treated as interchangeable when they are not.

### Subtle problems

- Effects on earnings are driven by composition changes in who remains employed.
- Wage effects are interpreted without enough attention to hours, bonuses, or formality.
- Mechanism claims about bargaining, search, or human capital are not distinguished from simple composition stories.

### What often raises the paper's ceiling

- Cleaner mapping from outcome to economic mechanism.
- Stronger discussion of incidence and equilibrium considerations.
- Better benchmarks for magnitudes relative to classic labor estimates.

## Education

### Common referee pressure points

- Test-score gains versus meaningful long-run outcomes.
- Selection into testing, grade progression, school choice, or track assignment.
- Treatment effects that may operate through peer composition, resources, or curriculum simultaneously.

### Subtle problems

- Short-run score effects are narrated as durable human-capital gains without supporting evidence.
- Administrative schooling outcomes shift because of reporting or institutional rules rather than learning.
- Heterogeneity by gender, region, or baseline ability looks ex post and unstructured.

### What often raises the paper's ceiling

- A clearer bridge from proximate outcomes to longer-run relevance.
- Better distinction between direct treatment effects and institutional re-sorting.
- More disciplined mechanism evidence around inputs, teacher behavior, or peer effects.

## Demography

### Common referee pressure points

- Timing versus level effects for fertility, marriage, migration, or mortality.
- Cohort composition and demographic selection.
- Administrative definitions of births, deaths, households, or marriages.

### Subtle problems

- The paper claims a level effect where the evidence is mostly timing displacement.
- Population denominator changes mechanically drive rates.
- Migration or household reclassification changes the sample in ways that mimic treatment effects.

### What often raises the paper's ceiling

- Clear decomposition of timing, intensive, and extensive margins.
- Better demographic accounting and denominator transparency.
- Stronger connection to population dynamics rather than isolated coefficient interpretation.

## Family

### Common referee pressure points

- Intra-household allocation versus household-level averages.
- Endogenous family formation, dissolution, and selection into observed household structure.
- Interpretation of outcomes for children, spouses, or caregivers when exposure differs within the household.

### Subtle problems

- Household-level outcomes mask redistribution within the family.
- The treatment changes who is observed in the household, not only outcomes within stable households.
- Mechanism claims about norms, bargaining, or caregiving are not distinguished from income or composition effects.

### What often raises the paper's ceiling

- Better alignment between the unit of theory and the unit of measurement.
- More careful decomposition of extensive-margin family changes versus within-family responses.
- Sharper mechanism tests tied to bargaining, childcare, or household production.

## Public

### Common referee pressure points

- Policy incidence, take-up, enforcement, and implementation heterogeneity.
- General equilibrium or fiscal spillovers.
- Whether reduced-form effects map cleanly to the policy margin of interest.

### Subtle problems

- The statutory policy differs from the implemented policy, but the paper treats them as identical.
- Take-up or enforcement is endogenous and under-discussed.
- Welfare claims outrun what the reduced form can support.

### What often raises the paper's ceiling

- Better institutional detail on implementation and compliance.
- Cleaner translation from treatment effect to policy quantity.
- A more disciplined welfare or incidence discussion.

## Development

### Common referee pressure points

- External validity across institutional contexts.
- Market spillovers, informal responses, and implementation variation.
- Measurement quality in surveys, administrative records, or self-reports.

### Subtle problems

- The paper oversells generalizability from a narrow context.
- Survey outcomes are treated as precise despite meaningful reporting risk.
- Mechanism stories rely on contextual assumptions that are plausible locally but not demonstrated.

### What often raises the paper's ceiling

- More explicit institutional boundary conditions.
- Better triangulation across outcomes or data sources.
- Stronger explanation of why the result should matter outside the study setting.

## Cross-Field Positioning Questions

- Is the contribution truly new for this field, or only new to a neighboring one?
- Which canonical papers will referees immediately compare this to?
- Is the policy, theory, or substantive relevance stated in the language of the field?
- Does the paper anticipate the standard field-specific objection before the referee raises it?
